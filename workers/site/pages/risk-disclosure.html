Trading and investing can result in loss, including loss of your entire investment and, in some circumstances, more than your initial deposit.

Market and execution risk

Prices can change quickly. An order may be rejected, partially filled, delayed or filled at a different price than expected. Liquidity may disappear, trading may halt, and a venue or service may be unavailable. Stop orders do not guarantee a particular execution price. System, network, data or configuration failures can cause missed or unintended actions.

Leverage and futures

Borrowing, margin, options and leveraged products amplify both gains and losses. You may owe more than the amount initially deposited and may face margin calls or forced liquidation. Futures are leveraged, expire or require roll decisions, and can move sharply; understand contract size, settlement, margin and delivery obligations before trading.

Cryptoassets

Crypto markets can be volatile, fragmented and subject to outages, custody failures, cyberattacks, protocol changes and changing law. Transactions may be irreversible. You may lose all value or access to assets.

Prediction markets

Prediction-market contracts may be restricted by jurisdiction, eligibility rules or venue terms. Outcomes depend on contract resolution rules and may be disputed. You can lose the full amount committed, and a market may be illiquid or unavailable.

Analytics, AI and simulations

Indicators, model outputs, trade certainty scores, AI responses, backtests and historical examples can be wrong, biased, overfit, incomplete or based on stale or licensed data with limitations. They are not probabilities of your personal outcome or promises of future performance. A past simulation omits some live-market, liquidity, fee, slippage, latency and behavioral effects.

Automation

Automated systems may act quickly and repeatedly, misunderstand inputs or continue under conditions you did not anticipate. Verify every configuration, venue, account, instrument, size and risk limit. Use paper or demo trading first, monitor activity, keep HALT available and never rely on automation as your only safeguard.

Use only funds you can afford to lose. This disclosure is general information, not investment, legal or tax advice. Consider obtaining independent professional advice.
